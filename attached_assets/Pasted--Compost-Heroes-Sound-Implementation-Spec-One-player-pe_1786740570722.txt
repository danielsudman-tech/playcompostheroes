# Compost Heroes — Sound Implementation Spec
*One player per device/computer. Multiplayer session synced across devices.*

---

## 1. Core Principle

Every sound event has a **single trigger and a single audio asset**. Whether a player hears it at full volume or ducked/muted depends only on one client-side check:

> **Is this the active player (whose turn it is)?**

Do not create separate "active" and "waiting" sound files — use one asset per event and apply a volume multiplier based on turn state. This keeps the sound system simple to build and easy to maintain.

---

## 2. Sound Event List

| # | Event | Trigger | Sound Feel | Active Player Volume | Other Players' Volume |
|---|-------|---------|-------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Draw a card | Player takes 1 card from Draw Pile | Light paper riffle/flip, short | 100% | 0% (silent) |
| 2 | Play a Compost Hero (new layer) | Hero placed on an empty layer slot in own pile | Warm organic "plop"/squish | 100% | 40–50% |
| 3 | Play a Compost Hero (stacking) | Hero placed on a layer that already has one | Layered double-thud, distinct from #2 | 100% | 40–50% |
| 4 | Immediate Action Card played | Action resolves, then discards | Quick zap/burst, punchy | 100% | 40–50% |
| 5 | Ongoing Action Card placed | Card placed in Ongoing Action slot | Rising "settle-in" sound | 100% | 90–100% (everyone needs this) |
| 6 | Ongoing Action Card replaced/cleared | New Ongoing card replaces old one | Short "clear/reset" sound | 100% | 90–100% |
| 7 | Ongoing effect active (ambient) | While an Ongoing card is in play | Subtle shift in background ambience | Continuous, low-level | Continuous, low-level, same for all |
| 8 | Layer completed (first hero on that layer) | Player gets first hero for one of the 5 layers | Layer-specific chime (5 unique tones) | 100% | 100% |
| 9 | Win — "I am a Compost Hero!" | Player completes all 5 layers | Full fanfare combining the 5 layer tones | 100% | 100% |
| 10 | Forced play / self-harm effect | Card forces player to damage own pile | Lower-pitched "uh-oh" | 100% | 60–70% (visible/audible to others) |
| 11 | Discard (hand limit) | Player discards down to 5 cards | Soft "flick," minimal | 100% | 0% (silent) |
| 12 | Deck refill | Draw Pile empty, Discard Pile reshuffled | Bigger shuffle sound | 100% | 20–30% (optional, low priority) |
| 13 | Turn passes to you | Game hands control to this player | Friendly notification cue | N/A — this always plays for the *new* active player | Not applicable |

## 3. Layer Tone System

Assign each of the 5 layers its own recognizable tone/instrument voice, used consistently across:
- Layer-complete chime (event #8)
- Contributing note in the win fanfare (event #9)

| Layer | Suggested Tone Direction |
|-------|---------------------------|
| Moisture | Light, flowing (e.g., water droplet / bell) |
| Carbon | Deep, woody (e.g., low wood block / marimba) |
| Decomposer | Organic, textured (e.g., soft rustle + tone) |
| Nitrogen | Bright, punchy (e.g., short brass/pluck) |
| Airflow | Airy, breathy (e.g., wind chime / flute) |

This reinforces the sorting logic through sound alone, not just card color/icon.

---

## 4. Notification Pop-ups + Sound

Keep existing pop-up notifications for other players' actions. Pair each pop-up with its ducked sound event (per volume table above) rather than adding new sounds — the pop-up text + quiet audio cue work together so waiting players stay oriented without full-volume noise stacking up across 3–4 players.

---

## 5. Build Notes

1. **Volume multiplier logic**, not duplicate assets — one sound file per event, scaled by `isActivePlayer` boolean.
2. **Ongoing ambience (event #7)** should be a single flexible track with 2–3 subtle variations, not a second competing ambient layer on top of a base ambient bed.
3. **Full-volume-for-everyone events** (#5, #6, #8, #9) are the exceptions — these carry game-state information relevant to all players and should cut through any ducked mix.
4. **Silent-for-others events** (#1, #11) are high-frequency, low-relevance actions — muting them for waiting players prevents audio clutter across a 4-player session.
5. **Mute toggle** (from earlier discussion) should mute all sound categories except pop-up-paired full-volume events if you want game-state awareness to persist even when audio is off — worth deciding whether ambient/hero sounds are the only thing muted, or everything.
6. Keep individual sound assets short and compressed (shared camp devices, variable wifi).

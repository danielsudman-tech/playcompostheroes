# Card Effects, Rule Support, and Board Visibility

The current online version already knows the full Compost Heroes card set and the features/effects associated with each card. Preserve and build on that logic.

Many Compost Heroes cards include a **yellow effect section** that explains what happens when the card is played. In this new facilitated multiplayer mode, the online system should actively help players carry out those effects correctly.

The goal is not simply to display the card text. The system should translate card effects into clear, contextual actions.

For example:

* If a card causes **Pile Flip**, the system should automatically shift each player's compost pile cards to the left according to the game rules and clearly show players what just happened.
* If a card allows a player to **steal a Nitrogen card from another player**, playing that card should trigger a simple selection step:

  * Choose which player to steal from.
  * If needed, choose the eligible Nitrogen card.
  * Confirm the move.
  * Show the card transferring to the correct compost pile.
* If a card requires selecting another player, choosing a card, drawing extra cards, discarding, moving a Hero, blocking an action, or resolving another specific effect, the system should present the appropriate interaction at that moment.

Think of each card effect as potentially having its own small guided workflow.

The player should never have to read a complicated effect and then manually figure out how to reproduce it in the interface.

Instead:

**Play card → system explains effect → player makes any required decisions → system executes the rules → play continues.**

The current game logic should be audited carefully to identify all cards that require special digital support.

Create an inventory of card-effect interaction types, such as:

* Draw additional cards
* Draw until a condition is met
* Steal a Hero/card
* Move or swap a card
* Shift compost piles
* Target another player
* Target a specific compost layer
* Prevent/block an action
* Apply an ongoing effect
* Remove an ongoing effect
* Force a discard
* Change hand size
* Affect all players
* Affect the next player
* Trigger another card or effect
* Require a player choice before resolving

For each interaction type, recommend the cleanest and most reusable UI pattern rather than creating a completely unique interface for every card.

## Yellow Card Effect Guidance

The yellow section of each card should become an important part of the digital experience.

When a player selects or plays a card, consider showing the effect in a highly readable way near the action area.

For example:

**Soggy Pile**

"Choose another player. Their compost pile is affected by excess moisture."

[Maria] [James] [Alex]

Then provide only the choices that are legal.

The interface should distinguish between:

* what the card says
* what the player needs to do now
* what the system will handle automatically

Avoid requiring players to interpret rule language when the software can guide them directly.

# Seeing the Other Players

Players need strong awareness of the other three compost piles because much of the strategy depends on understanding what opponents have collected and who may be close to winning.

Each player's board should show the important state of their compost pile at a glance.

A player should be able to quickly see:

* which compost layers another player has
* which layers they are missing
* important Heroes/cards currently in their pile
* ongoing effects affecting them
* how close they are to winning

Because individual cards contain useful details, explore an interaction where players can **click/tap another player's compost pile to enlarge it**.

The enlarged view could show:

* cards at a readable size
* Hero names
* compost categories
* card effects
* active restrictions/effects

This should be easy to open and dismiss without disrupting gameplay.

Do not permanently dedicate large amounts of screen space to full-size opponent cards if a compact overview + expandable inspection provides a cleaner experience.

---

# Shared Table Elements

Players should also be able to clearly see the shared game state, including:

* Draw pile
* Discard pile
* Ongoing Action card/effect area
* Current player's turn
* Current turn step
* Recent action or game event

The draw pile should feel like a recognizable deck that can be clicked when drawing is allowed.

The discard pile should allow players to understand what has recently been played/discarded where relevant, potentially with an expandable view if useful.

Ongoing Action cards need to remain visually present because their effects can influence future turns. They should not disappear into an activity feed where players may forget them.

Consider whether ongoing actions belong in a dedicated shared area of the table with a concise description of the active effect.

# Screen-Space Challenge

There is a lot of information competing for limited screen space:

* player's hand
* player's compost pile
* three opponents and their compost piles
* draw pile
* discard pile
* ongoing Action cards
* turn guidance
* card instructions
* activity/history
* game controls

Treat this as a major design challenge.

I want the interface to feel **clean, calm, and understandable**, not like every piece of information has been placed on screen simultaneously.

Explore hierarchy carefully.

Prioritize what a player needs:

### Always visible

* My hand
* My compost progress
* Whose turn it is
* Current turn step
* Opponent compost progress
* Draw pile
* Important ongoing effects

### Available when needed

* Full opponent card details
* Full discard history
* Full rules
* Detailed card explanations
* Previous actions
* Help/tutorial information

Use progressive disclosure wherever possible.

The interface should answer these questions immediately:

**Whose turn is it?**

**What am I supposed to do now?**

**What cards can I play?**

**What am I trying to collect?**

**How close are the other players to winning?**

**Is there an ongoing effect changing the rules right now?**

Everything else can be secondary.

# Responsive Layout Exploration

Explore different board layouts before settling on one.

Potential concepts could include:

### Digital Table

My hand along the bottom, my compost pile directly above it, opponents around the top/sides, shared piles in the center.

### Player-Focused Board

My board takes most of the space while opponents appear as smaller expandable compost summaries.

### Adaptive Turn Layout

The interface temporarily emphasizes whichever board/player is relevant to the current action.

For example, if I play a card that steals Nitrogen, the opponent area could expand temporarily to help me choose a target.

Do not assume a traditional four-sided card-table layout is necessarily best, especially on laptops with limited vertical space.

Optimize primarily for desktop/laptop use during Zoom or Teams-based corporate events, while keeping reasonable responsiveness for tablets and smaller screens.

---

# Critical Principle

The digital system should automate **rule execution**, not **player decision-making**.

If a card has an automatic mechanical consequence, the application can perform it.

If a card requires a strategic choice, the player should make that choice.

A strong card interaction should therefore feel like:

**I choose the card → the game tells me what decision is required → I make the decision → the system correctly performs the effect.**

That balance should guide the entire new mode.
